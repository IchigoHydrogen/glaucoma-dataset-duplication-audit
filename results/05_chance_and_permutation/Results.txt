================================================================================================
[2] Permutation nulls
================================================================================================
    Note: the number of exact matches depends only on the multiset of fingerprints.
    Permuting whole records does not change that multiset, so the count never moves.
    Only shuffling fields independently moves it, and that destroys their association.

    observed matches = 1302
    [check] permuting whole records gives 1302 matches, unchanged

    mean deviation shuffled alone (the null used at submission)  (B = 2000)
      median 3.0   95th centile 7.0   maximum 11   (mean 3.36, minimum 0)
      the observed 1302 exceeds the null maximum of 11. one-sided P < 0.0005

    mean deviation and age shuffled independently  (B = 2000)
      median 1.0   95th centile 4.0   maximum 7   (mean 1.60, minimum 0)
      the observed 1302 exceeds the null maximum of 7. one-sided P < 0.0005

    all 5 fields shuffled independently  (B = 2000)
      median 1.0   95th centile 4.0   maximum 8   (mean 1.57, minimum 0)
      the observed 1302 exceeds the null maximum of 8. one-sided P < 0.0005

    All three nulls are biased the same way. Shuffling fields independently breaks their
    association, enlarges the effective fingerprint space, and so yields fewer collisions.
    They are therefore lower bounds on the cross-release collision count, not expectations.

================================================================================================
[3] The cross-release collision rate, measured rather than modelled
================================================================================================
    All 1302 fingerprint-matched pairs were compared at the image level.
      image-identical (true duplicates)  1031
      different images (collisions)      271

    Stage 06 excludes fellow eyes and repeat visits as explanations for these pairs.
    They are different patients who happen to share a demographic fingerprint,
    which means the cross-release collision count has been observed directly.

    observed collisions        271 in 31,120,000 pairs  -> rate 8.708e-06
    predicted from within-release rate  1.87      -> rate 6.001e-08
    understatement factor      145x
    ratio of rates             145x

    The concern raised in comment 15 is correct, and its size can be measured, not just
    conceded: the within-release rate understates the cross-release rate by two orders.
    This does not weaken the duplication finding. The 1031 pairs rest on image comparison,
    not on the fingerprint, and two different eyes do not carry identical bytes.
    The 271 non-identical pairs reach a maximum cosine of 0.8584, while
    the 1031 identical pairs fall no lower than 0.999945; no pair lies between.
